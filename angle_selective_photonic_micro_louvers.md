### 1. The Midday Solar Absorption Bottleneck
In Western Rajasthan (Phalodi, Barmer, Pokhran), peak summer solar irradiance regularly reaches $G_{solar} > 1,020\,\text{W/m}^2$ at solar noon (zenith angle $\theta_z \approx 4^\circ - 12^\circ$). 

Under standard isotropic passive daytime radiative cooling coatings (such as porous P(VdF-HFP) or $BaSO_4$ paints):
* **The Residual Absorption Dilemma:** Even with an exceptional solar reflectance of $R_{solar} = 97.0\%$, the remaining $3.0\%$ parasitic absorption introduces:
$$P_{absorbed} = (1 - R_{solar}) \cdot G_{solar} = 0.03 \times 1,020\,\text{W/m}^2 \approx 30.6\,\text{W/m}^2$$
* Because theoretical maximum net cooling power through the $8 - 13\,\mu\text{m}$ atmospheric window is typically $60 - 80\,\text{W/m}^2$, this $30.6\,\text{W/m}^2$ solar heat load destroys roughly **$40\% - 50\%$ of the net cooling capacity**, capping daytime sub-ambient temperature drop to $\Delta T \le 6 - 8\,\text{K}$.

### 3. Net Daytime Radiative Cooling Thermodynamics

The net cooling power balance under direct desert noon sun ($1,020\,\text{W/m}^2$) is governed by:
$$P_{net}(T) = P_{rad}(T) - P_{atm}(T_{amb}) - P_{solar} - P_{non-rad}$$
where:
* $P_{rad}(T) = 2\pi \int_0^{\pi/2} d\theta \sin\theta \cos\theta \int_0^\infty I_{BB}(\lambda, T) \varepsilon(\lambda, \theta) d\lambda$
* $P_{solar} = (1 - R_{solar}) \cdot G_{solar} = (1 - 0.9972) \times 1,020\,\text{W/m}^2 = 2.85\,\text{W/m}^2$
* $P_{non-rad} = h_c (T_{amb} - T) \approx 6.5\,\text{W/(m}^2\cdot\text{K)} \times (T_{amb} - T)$
